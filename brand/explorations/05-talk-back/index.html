Motet brand exploration. Off-white ground, black borders, hard offsets, no gradients. One voice in cobalt, one in sunflower.

The idea

Motet turns content you trust into an interactive podcast you can listen to on the go.

Every other podcast is a monologue. Motet is the one you can talk to. It takes the newsletters, bookmarks and threads you already chose to follow — the content you trust because you picked it — and sings them into one episode you can listen to on a walk, a commute, a run. And it keeps a microphone open the whole time: when a story grabs you, say so, mid-sentence, and it goes deeper on that one, then picks the episode back up. A motet is several texts sung at once and made coherent; here, your voice is one of the texts.

The visual language follows from that: two voices, two flat colours, hard black edges so nothing blurs into anything else, and a speech-bubble tail on the mark because the product is a thing you talk to.

Cobalt and sunflower are a pair, never a scale. Coral appears once per screen at most — the live dot, an unread count — and never as a fill behind text. No tints, no gradients; if something needs to recede, it gets a dotted border instead of a lighter colour.
