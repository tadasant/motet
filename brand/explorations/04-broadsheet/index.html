Infrastructure

Fast software, slow rails

From @patrickc

The gap between how quickly software can be written and how quickly the institutions around it can move has never been wider. A payments stack that once took a year to stand up now takes an afternoon; the compliance review still takes the year. Most of the friction left in the economy is no longer technical.

That is a hopeful reading, because institutional problems are the kind that get solved once enough people find them embarrassing, and embarrassment is now very cheap to produce.

The interesting builders this decade will be the ones who treat a form as a bug.

Markets

The cut everyone saw coming

From Money Stuff

Here is a fun thing about a rate cut: the day before it happens, everyone agrees it is going to happen, and then it happens, and the market moves anyway. Prices are supposed to reflect expectations, but expectations turn out to include a small, stubborn probability that the thing everyone expects will not occur, and that probability has to go somewhere when it goes to zero.

Anyway, the bond desks had a nice afternoon, and someone, somewhere, is explaining to a risk committee why they were short the obvious.

The paper, read aloud

Motet turns content you trust into an interactive podcast you can listen to on the go.

Motet is your personal morning paper, delivered as a podcast: the newsletters, bookmarks and threads you already chose to follow, set as one edition and read to you on the go. Every story in it is there because you trust the byline, not because we picked it. And the podcast is interactive, because any story continues on the next page — say so out loud, and Motet goes deeper on it, then picks the episode back up where it left off. The craft of print — a masthead, kickers, hairline rules, columns, a dateline — shown turning into sound. Polyphony is the newspaper’s oldest trick: many bylines, one edition.

Timeless and high-contrast. Serious without being stuffy. Every visual device is a device print already uses — the source line under each headline, the “continued on p.2” jump — and the only thing new on the page is the playhead.
