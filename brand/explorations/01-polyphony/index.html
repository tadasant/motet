Polyphony

Motet turns content you trust into an interactive podcast you can listen to on the go.

A motet is a choral form in which several texts are sung at once and resolve into one coherent piece. Motet the product does the same with the newsletters, bookmarks and threads you chose to follow: many voices you already trust, sung together into one podcast you take with you — and can talk to. When a story catches you, you say so out loud; Motet goes deeper on it, then picks the episode back up. The brand is cultured, editorial and warm — parchment and ink with four voice colours that only ever appear together — a score, not a dashboard, and never a newspaper.

Parchment and ink carry ninety-five percent of every surface. The four voices are a chord, not a set of options: they appear together in the motif, in the hairline under the primary action, and in small accents that mark a source — never as section colours, never one at a time on a large area.

How it behavesFour rules
  • iVoices only sing together. A single voice hue on its own reads as a category label; four at once read as Motet.
  • iiInk speaks, colour points. Headlines, body and the resolved line are ink. Colour marks which of your sources is singing.
  • iiiCurves, not corners. Ribbons, round caps, soft serifs. Nothing in the system is a hard-edged bar.
  • ivWhitespace is the acoustic. Generous margins are the cathedral; cramp the layout and the polyphony turns to noise.

Tuesday, twenty-two minutes.

Three newsletters and eleven bookmarks you trust covered the same funding round. Motet tells it once, on your walk, in a few sentences. — you: tell me more about that. It goes deeper on the round, then picks the episode back up where it left off.

Instrument Sans carries the interface and every paragraph that is meant to be scanned. It is plain on purpose: the display face is the singer, the body face is the programme note.
